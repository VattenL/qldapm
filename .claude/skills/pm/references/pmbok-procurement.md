decisions a project manager makes in this area, with the PMBOK name a proposed remedy has to carry.

## Chapter 12 terms worth using, with the book's meaning

- **Procurement** (PM 12 p459/498): purchasing or acquiring products, services or results from outside the project
  team, plus the management and control of the agreements (contracts, purchase orders, MOAs, internal SLAs).
- **Buyer, seller** (p461/500): the seller may be called contractor, vendor, service provider or supplier, and is
  first a bidder, then the selected source, then the contracted supplier. The chapter is written from the viewpoint of
  obtaining goods or services from outside the project (p459/498); the seller's own view is only sketched (p462/501).
- **Agreement or contract** (PM 12.2.3.2 p489/528): mutually binding; obliges the seller to provide and the buyer to pay;
  subject to remedy in the courts. "Anything not in the contract cannot be legally enforced", knowledge transfer
  included (p461/500). The project manager is typically not authorized to sign binding agreements (p460/499), except
  in decentralized purchasing, where the project manager may negotiate and sign (p462/501).
- **Fixed-price, cost-reimbursable, time and material** (PM 12.1.1.6 p471/510 to 472/511): FFP, FPIF, FPEPA;
  CPFF, CPIF, CPAF; T&M. **Delivery method, contract payment type, procurement phases** (12.1.3.2 p476/515): what the
  strategy decides. Contract payment types are separate from delivery methods.
- **Bid documents** (12.1.3.3 p477/516): RFI, RFQ, RFP. **Procurement SOW** (12.1.3.4 p477/516): only the part of
  the scope baseline placed in this contract. **TOR**: the SOW's name for services (p478/517).
- **Long-lead item** (p464/503): ordered early because manufacture and delivery drive the schedule; in IT "may
  require ordering 2 to 3 months in advance". **Trial engagement** (p464/503): several candidate sellers paid for
  initial deliverables before the larger commitment.
- **Claim, dispute, appeal, constructive change** (PM 12.3.2.2 p498/537): a contested change is a claim; unresolved
  it becomes a dispute and then an appeal; negotiation is the preferred settlement; otherwise alternative dispute
  resolution (ADR) per the contract. A **constructive change** is a requested change one party treats as a change
  and the other disputes (12.3.3.4 p499/538).

## Traps

- **Whose procurement.** The forms are the buyer's. The supplier-center contract is the project's sale, not a purchase
  by the project, and the book prints no seller-side forms (PM p462/501 sketches the seller view). Fill these
  forms for the real purchases (hosting, gateway, store accounts, data entry, tools) and say so in the reading paragraph.
- **The center signs three of them.** The project team may have no contracting authority, and the roles box must show
  that. A duty the project keeps over a center-held account (2.37 Integration) is coordination, not contract
  administration.
- **The blank form wins over the prose table.** Table 2.33 says "Roles, responsibilities, and authority" and "Prequalified
  sellers"; the form prints "Roles, Responsibility, and Authority" and "Prequalified Sellers". PMBOK writes FPEPA; the
  form prints FP-EPA. Table 2.34 has sub-elements the form does not print. Use the printed labels (`fields/*.json`).
- **Tailoring applies here; name it.** Few procurements: combine 2.37 with 2.38 (BoF p148/159, p152/163). A small
  simple procurement needs no weighted 2.39 (p155/166). A small contract can fold its vendor analysis into one
  paragraph and skip changes and disputes (BoF 4.8 p222/233). 2.37 "is developed once" (p147/158); 2.38 per procurement.
- **Fixed price, no cost forecast.** 4.6 Forecast Performance: "If the contract is a fixed price, do not enter cost
  forecasts" (BoF p214/225). Schedule and delivery forecasts are still written.
- **4.8 is closed by conditions, not by date.** All disputes resolved, product accepted, final payments made (BoF
  p222/233); PMBOK adds no outstanding claims or invoices and deliverables approved (PM p499/538). The final invoice is
  also charter exit criterion 10.
- **2.39 Totals row.** Its Rating cells print solid black and are not filled; Weight and Score cells stay white
  (fields notes). **Table 12-1 is not prescriptive** (PM p481/520): add no SOW or RFP box to 2.37. 4.6 Risks and
  Issues must also be in the risk register and issue log (BoF p214/225): one ID, one text.

## Formulas the chapter and the Book give

Chapter 12 states no equation and no numeric threshold. The only arithmetic is on 2.39 (BoF steps 2, 6, 7 p155/166;
Table 2.35 p156/167); the earned value equations chapter 12 names (PM 12.3.2.3 p498/537) are chapter 7's.

- Weights: the sum of the weights of all criteria = 100 percent (weight in percent).
- Score = Weight x Rating (weight per criterion; rating on the chosen scale, "such as 1-5 or 1-10", p155/166).
- Total (a candidate) = the sum of that candidate's Scores; the highest total is the winner (p155/166). The book does
  not say whether a 20 percent weight is entered as 20 or 0.2; state which in the reading paragraph.
- CV = EV - AC; SV = EV - PV; CPI = EV/AC; SPI = EV/PV; EAC = BAC/CPI; VAC = BAC - EAC (PM 7.4.2.2 p262/301 to
  265/304; Table 7-1 p267/306; EV, PV, AC, BAC, EAC in the contract's currency; CPI, SPI are ratios). `pmbok-cost.md`
  Formulas holds the full set.
- CPIF sharing is an example, not a formula: "for example, an 80/20 split over/under target costs" (PM p472/511). FPIF
  has a price ceiling above which the seller bears the cost (p471/510); the ceiling is a contract term, no value stated.

## Decisions in this area

Where a decision shows no Formula bullet, the book gives none. Record each decision in 3.2 Decision Log (ID, Category,
Decision, Responsible Party, Date, Comments; BoF p169/180). A decision that changes a baseline, the scope placed in a
contract, or the contract itself goes to 3.3 Change Request, which PM 12.1.3.8 (p479/518) and 12.3.3.4 (p499/538)
route through Perform Integrated Change Control.

### 1. Make or buy

- **Arises:** 12.1, once or at predefined points (PM p466/505); before any seller is approached. Trigger: a need that the five-person team cannot cover or should not.
- **Techniques and trade-offs:** make-or-buy analysis (p473/512). Factors: current resource allocation and skills, need for specialized expertise, wish not to expand permanent employment obligations, need for independent expertise, and the risks of each choice. It may use payback period, ROI, IRR, discounted cash flow, NPV or benefit/cost analysis; no criterion picks among them and no equation is given. Market research on seller capabilities (p473/512). Expert judgment in procurement, contract types and compliance (p472/511).
- **Inputs, where kept:** charter funds and exclusions; scope baseline; resource requirements (scope package Part 3); risk register; business case (charter cash case). Cost of managing the procurement belongs in the cost baseline (PM p484/523).
- **Records:** 3.2 (the make-or-buy decision, PM 12.1.3.6 p479/518); 2.37 Assumptions and Constraints; 3.3 if the decision adds a purchase outside the baseline (p479/518).
- **Wrong move:** comparing price only. Reason: the analysis weighs skills, specialized and independent expertise, and the risk of each option (p473/512), and a purchase also costs managing (p484/523).

### 2. Contract type and incentive

- **Arises:** 12.1, strategy (PM 12.1.3.2 p476/515), after the decision to buy.
- **Techniques and trade-offs:** fixed-price (FFP: price set at the outset, favored by buyers, changes only if the scope changes; FPIF: flexibility with incentives tied to cost, schedule or technical performance and a price ceiling above which the seller pays; FPEPA: for long periods or a different currency, predefined adjustments for inflation or commodity costs) when "requirements are well defined and no significant changes to the scope are expected". Cost-reimbursable (CPFF: fee a percentage of initial estimated cost, unchanged unless scope changes; CPIF: predetermined incentive, costs shared by a prenegotiated formula; CPAF: most of the fee on subjective criteria, decided by the buyer, "generally not subject to appeals") when the scope is expected to change significantly. T&M: hybrid, for staff augmentation, experts, or when a precise SOW cannot be written quickly (pp471 to 472/510 to 511). Types may be combined. Incentives and awards align buyer and seller objectives (p476/515).
- **Inputs, where kept:** requirements stability (assumption 13 and R2: rules frozen at M1); the SOW; R5, R12.
- **Records:** 2.38 Contract Type checkboxes and the Incentive or Award Fee table (Criteria); 3.2. No document names a type for any of the four purchases; ask.
- **Wrong move:** a fixed price for work whose requirements are still evolving. Reason: fixed price suits predictable, well-defined work that is not likely to change; cost plus suits evolving work (p476/515).

### 3. Delivery method, phases, number of sellers, trial engagement

- **Arises:** 12.1 strategy; revisited in 12.2 when proposals arrive (PM p485/524).
- **Techniques and trade-offs:** delivery methods for professional services (no subcontracting, subcontracting allowed, joint venture, representative); procurement phases with exit criteria and knowledge transfer (p476/515). Signing more than one seller mitigates damage from one seller's failure (p485/524); the cost of doing so is not stated. A trial engagement pays several candidates for initial deliverables: it speeds momentum and lets the buyer evaluate partners while progressing (p464/503); its price is not stated. A governing agreement such as an MSA with adaptive work in an appendix lets adaptive scope change without changing the contract (p465/504).
- **Inputs, where kept:** milestone list (charter summary milestone schedule M0 to M7), schedule (scope package Due Dates), resource requirements.
- **Records:** 2.38 Delivery Method and Procurement Life Cycle (Phase, Entry Criteria, Key Deliverables or Milestones, Exit Criteria, Knowledge Transfer); 3.2.
- **Wrong move:** omitting knowledge transfer. Reason: the contract should state it (p461/500), and the project exit criteria require handover to the center (charter exit criteria 6 and 7).

### 4. Selection method

- **Arises:** 12.1 source selection analysis (PM p473/512); the method is included in the procurement documents so bidders know it.
- **Techniques and trade-offs:** least cost (standard, routine, well-defined outcome); qualifications only (value small, so a full selection would not pay); quality-based or highest technical proposal score (technical first, then negotiate the price of the top-ranked); quality and cost-based (when risk or uncertainty is greater for the project, quality "should be a key element when compared to cost"); sole source (no competition, acceptable only when justified, an exception); fixed budget (budget disclosed, sellers adapt scope and quality to it; only when the SOW is precise, no change is anticipated and the budget cannot be exceeded) (pp473 to 474/512 to 513). First review the priority of the competing demands (p473/512).
- **Inputs, where kept:** charter objectives and constraints (scope, schedule, cost, quality criteria); the value of each purchase (charter line 2); R5.
- **Records:** 2.38 (reading paragraph names the method); 2.39 when weights are used; 3.2 for a sole-source justification.
- **Wrong move:** a fixed budget with an imprecise SOW. Reason: sellers then shrink scope or quality to fit, and the book allows the method only when the SOW is precise and unchanged (p474/513).

### 5. Criteria, weights and rating scale

- **Arises:** 12.1, for each major procurement (BoF 2.39 p155/166); applied in 12.2 proposal evaluation (PM p487/526).
- **Techniques and trade-offs:** weighting system with a numerical score, colour code or written description (PM p479/518); the BoF steps: determine criteria, weights summing to 100 percent, a rating range such as 1-5 or 1-10, the performance each rating needs, rate each proposal, multiply, total (p155/166). Include product cost and life cycle cost, not price alone (p478/517). A price proposal separate from the technical one is good practice (p486/525). The book gives no rule for choosing the weights themselves.
- **Inputs, where kept:** requirements documentation and scope baseline (charter 1.1 to 1.3; scope package), procurement strategy; the second provider evaluation 1.2.3.2-A3 is the project's one competitive step. No weights exist in `docs/`.
- **Records:** 2.39 Criterion rows, rating definitions, Weight, per-candidate Rating and Score, Totals; 3.2 for the weight decision.
- **Formula:** Sum of Weights = 100 percent; Score = Weight x Rating; Total = sum of the candidate's Scores.
- **Wrong move:** setting weights after seeing proposals. Reason: good practice puts the evaluation method in the procurement documents so bidders know how they will be judged (p473/512).

### 6. Whether to use an independent estimate

- **Arises:** 12.1, for large procurements (PM 12.1.3.7 p479/518).
- **Techniques and trade-offs:** the buyer prepares its own estimate or has an outside professional estimator prepare one, as a benchmark; a significant difference may mean the SOW is deficient or ambiguous or the sellers misunderstood it. Cost of the estimate versus its value, and the size of a "significant" difference: not stated.
- **Inputs, where kept:** charter budget lines; cost estimate worksheets (scope package Part 3).
- **Records:** 2.37 Independent Estimates (used or not, and needed for source selection or not); 3.2.
- **Wrong move:** accepting the lowest bid without a benchmark. Reason: the independent estimate is the reasonableness check against proposals (p485/524).

### 7. Bid document, advertising, bidder conference

- **Arises:** 12.1 outputs; 12.2 execution (PM p487/526).
- **Techniques and trade-offs:** RFI when more information on goods or services is needed (followed by RFQ or RFP); RFQ when more information on how vendors meet the requirements and the cost is needed; RFP when the problem is hard and the solution unclear, the most formal, with strict rules for content, timeline and responses. Bid, tender or quotation suit price-based choices, proposal suits technical choices (p477/516). Detail consistent with value and risk (p477/516). Advertising widens the seller list (p487/526); a bidder conference gives all bidders one understanding and no preferential treatment (p487/526). A preapproved seller list shortens the process (p471/510).
- **Inputs, where kept:** SOW (from the scope baseline); stakeholder register (charter key stakeholder list names the gateway and hosting providers).
- **Records:** no form for bid documents; 2.37 Timing (date proposals are due, SOW complete; BoF p149/160) and Prequalified Sellers.
- **Wrong move:** an RFP for a routine purchase, or an RFQ for an unclear problem. Reason: the document type follows the question being asked (p477/516).

### 8. Risk allocation, bonds and insurance

- **Arises:** 12.1 (risk register is an input and an updated document, p470/509 and p480/519); 12.2 as each seller adds risks (p491/530).
- **Techniques and trade-offs:** write the contract to allocate each risk to the party most capable of managing it, since "no contractor is capable of managing all the possible major risks"; the buyer keeps the risks outside the contractor's control (p463/502). Performance bonds or insurance mitigate some risk (p475/514). Some risks are transferred by agreement (p470/509). The cost of a bond or an insurance cover is not stated. Response strategy names are chapter 11 (`pmbok-risk.md`).
- **Inputs, where kept:** charter R5, R6, R12 and the stakeholder list.
- **Records:** 2.37 Risk Management and the Integration row Risk; 2.32 Response, Actions, Revised columns; 3.3 if a response changes a baseline.
- **Wrong move:** treating the seller as carrying every risk. Reason: the buyer has to accept what no contractor controls (p463/502). A seller also brings its own risks (p480/519).

### 9. Roles, signing authority, who negotiates

- **Arises:** 12.1, "early" (PM p468/507); negotiation in 12.2 (p488/527).
- **Techniques and trade-offs:** roles of the project manager, the procurement department, legal and others are documented in 2.37; the project team is staffed with the expertise needed (p468/507). Decentralized purchasing lets the project manager negotiate and sign; centralized puts it in a department (p462/501). Negotiation is led by a member of the procurement team who has authority to sign; the project manager may assist (p488/527). Complex, high-value, high-risk awards need senior management approval (p488/527).
- **Inputs, where kept:** charter 2A authority (the limit per transaction; contract employment decisions with the supplier's line management); the Agreement Information rows (the center signs).
- **Records:** 2.37 Roles, Responsibility, and Authority; 3.2; 1.3 for contracting and legal staff (PM p480/519).
- **Wrong move:** the project manager signing a binding agreement. Reason: the project manager is typically not authorized to (p460/499).

### 10. Timing and long-lead items

- **Arises:** 12.1, because schedule requirements shape the strategy and procurement decisions shape the schedule (PM p468/507).
- **Techniques and trade-offs:** integrate with Develop Schedule and Estimate Activity Resources (p468/507); order long-lead items early, even before final design, from top-level requirements (p464/503); a preapproved seller list shortens selection (p471/510). The risk of ordering before final design is not stated.
- **Inputs, where kept:** milestone list; scope package Due Dates; Apple enrolment up to four weeks, opened at M1 (R12); cloud 19 to 23 Oct 2026.
- **Records:** 2.37 Timing of Key Procurement Activities (Date, Activity) and Integration row Schedule; milestone list update (p480/519); 3.3 when a purchase date moves the schedule baseline (p490/529).
- **Wrong move:** buying after the work needs it. Reason: the milestone list shows when sellers must deliver (p469/508) and long-lead items drive the schedule (p464/503).

### 11. Payment terms, amendments, and agreement content

- **Arises:** 12.2 agreement drafting; 12.3 payments and amendments.
- **Techniques and trade-offs:** agreement components list (p489/528): SOW, schedule, reporting, price and payment, inspection and acceptance, warranty, incentives and penalties, insurance and bonds, subcontractor approval, terms, change handling, termination and ADR. Payments should track work accomplished; a contract that links payments to output and deliverables rather than inputs such as labor hours has better controls (p494/533). Amend only by mutual consent under the change terms, in writing (p494/533).
- **Inputs, where kept:** acceptance records (charter 2A approval table); budget tranches.
- **Records:** 2.37 Performance Metrics; 3.4 Change Log for each amendment (copied to 4.8 Record of Contract Changes); 3.3.
- **Wrong move:** acting on verbal direction. Reason: all procurement-related changes are written and approved before implementation (p496/535).

### 12. Respond to a seller variance, claim or change

- **Arises:** 12.3, each reporting period (4.6) or on a seller delay or dispute.
- **Techniques and trade-offs:** performance reviews, earned value analysis, trend analysis, inspection (PM 12.3.2.3 to 12.3.2.4 p498/537); claims administration, with negotiation preferred and ADR otherwise (p498/537). A seller delay that affects overall performance may need the schedule baseline updated and approved; watch the cascade to other sellers (p500/539). Price changes go into the cost baseline (p500/539). Which remedy is cheapest is not stated.
- **Inputs, where kept:** 4.6 reports; agreements; quality reports; work performance data; schedule and cost baselines.
- **Records:** 4.6 Planned Corrective or Preventive Action and Claims or Disputes; 3.1 Issue Log; 3.3 and 3.4; 4.3 and 4.4.
- **Formula:** CV = EV - AC; SV = EV - PV; CPI = EV/AC; SPI = EV/PV (see Formulas).
- **Wrong move:** leaving a constructive change unrecorded. Reason: it can be disputed and become a claim, so it is identified and documented in correspondence (p499/538).

### 13. Audit the procurement process

- **Arises:** 12.3, periodic or as needed (BoF 4.7 p218/229).
- **Techniques and trade-offs:** audits, a structured review of the procurement process; the rights and obligations are described in the contract; observations go to both project managers (PM 12.3.2.5 p498/537). The "independence and credibility of procurement audits" is critical to the system's reliability (p494/533). Quality audits are 8.2.2.5 (`pmbok-quality.md`). The audit's cost is not stated.
- **Inputs, where kept:** 2.37, 4.6 reports, contract file; no audit is funded in `docs/`. **Records:** 4.7 Procurement Audit (including Followed per process); 3.2; 5.1.
- **Wrong move:** an audit by the person who ran the procurement. Reason: independence is what makes the audit credible (p494/533).

### 14. Close the contract

- **Arises:** 12.3 outputs, when work and payments end (PM p499/538).
- **Techniques and trade-offs:** closure conditions in the contract and the plan; the buyer's written notice; seller performance evaluation, prequalified list update, lessons learned archive, and the procurement file (p501/540). Project closeout may absorb 4.8 when one contract drives the project (BoF p222/233).
- **Inputs, where kept:** 3.4 change log; dispute record; acceptance records; final invoice (charter exit criterion 10). **Records:** 4.8 Contract Closeout Report; 5.1; 3.2.
- **Wrong move:** closing with a claim or an invoice open. Reason: closure requires no outstanding claims or invoices and all final payments made (p499/538).
