Charter, 2.28 Resource Requirements, 2.29 Resource Breakdown Structure, 3.7 Team Performance Assessment or
4.1 Team Member Status Report. The forms print the boxes; chapter 9 says what goes in them and which
technique decides each choice. A remedy carries the PMBOK name of its technique and the book's trade-off.

## Chapter 9 terms, with the book's meaning

- **Team and physical resources** (printed 309, PDF 348): personnel versus equipment, materials, facilities,
  infrastructure. Control Resources (9.6) covers physical resources only; people are Manage Team (9.5)
  (printed 353, PDF 392).
- **Role, authority, responsibility, competence** (9.1.3.1, printed 318 to 319, PDF 357 to 358): authority is
  the right to apply resources, decide, sign approvals, accept deliverables, and should match responsibility; a
  competence gap is met by training, hiring, schedule changes or scope changes.
- **RAM, RACI** (9.1.2.2, printed 316 to 317, PDF 355 to 356): the matrix shows all activities of one person and
  all people on one activity and ensures only one person is accountable for any task.
- **Resource requirements** (9.2.3.1, printed 325, PDF 364): types and quantities per work package or activity,
  aggregated per work package, WBS branch and project, with assumptions. **Basis of estimates** (9.2.3.2,
  printed 326, PDF 365): method, sources, assumptions, constraints, range, confidence level, risks.
- **Resource breakdown structure** (9.2.3.3, printed 326 to 327, PDF 365 to 366): resources by category (labor,
  material, equipment, supplies) and type (skill level, grade, certification); a guide in 9.1, the completed
  document in 9.2, used to acquire and monitor.
- **Pre-assignment** (9.3.2.3, printed 333, PDF 372): resources fixed in advance, by a proposal, by dependence on
  named persons, or in the charter. **Resource calendar** (9.3.3.3, printed 334, PDF 373): when each resource is
  available; progressively elaborated.
- **Team charter** (9.1.3.2, printed 319 to 320, PDF 358 to 359); **team performance assessment** (9.4.3.1,
  printed 343, PDF 382); **work performance reports** (9.5.1.3, printed 347, PDF 386).

## Traps

- Charter 1.1.3's seven roles are users of the software, not project roles. 2.25 columns and 2.26 roles are
  PM and BA, developers, QA, mobile developer and the customer-side business owners.
- 2.26 Training Requirements is training the team. Work package 1.8.2.2 trains the center's 52 staff, a deliverable.
- The dictionary's "Responsible Person" is the single accountable owner (scope package decomposition method),
  while the 2.25 legend says R performs and A answers for the work. Decide once which letter it takes.
- Dictionary hours are effort, not loading. 2.28 Quantity counts units; weekly load comes from 2.18.
- Blank form wins over prose: 2.28 "Resource" not "Type of resource"; 2.26 boxes; 3.7 has no Cohesiveness; 4.1
  labels. 3.7 rates the team as a whole; rating individuals is a tailoring (printed 184, PDF 195); the book gives
  no rating criteria.
- The 2.17 equation and its text disagree (D9).
- A baseline change is never an edit: 9.3.3.4 and 9.3.3.5 (printed 334, PDF 373), 9.4.3.2 (printed 343, PDF 382),
  9.5.3.1 (printed 350, PDF 389) and 9.6.3.2 (printed 357, PDF 396) all say change request.
- The mobile developer joins after M2: include a contractor or newcomer in the team charter (form 2.27 tips,
  printed 109, PDF 120; PMBOK printed 320, PDF 359).
- The reserve budget line is a contingency reserve, never a management reserve (`house-style.md`).

## Decisions in this area

Per decision: when; techniques and stated trade-offs; inputs and where kept; where recorded; formula; wrong
move. "Not stated in the book" means no criterion: the team's choice and reason go in 3.2 Decision Log (ID,
Category, Decision, Responsible Party, Date, Comments; PDF page 180, printed page 169).

### D1. Format and level of roles and the RAM

- **When:** 9.1 at planning start; elaborated as scope firms (form 2.25, printed 101, PDF 112).
- **Techniques:** hierarchical (WBS, OBS, RBS), matrix (RAM, RACI), text (position descriptions); hierarchical
  for high-level roles, text for detail (printed 316, PDF 355). Level: work package, control account or activity;
  large projects WBS by OBS, small ones deliverable or activity (printed 101 to 102, PDF 112 to 113). Cost of
  detail: not stated in the book.
- **Inputs:** WBS and dictionary, requirements, stakeholder register 1.3, charter key stakeholders.
- **Recorded:** 2.25 grid; 2.26 Roles, Responsibilities, and Authority, Project Organizational Structure.
- **Formula:** none.
- **Wrong move:** a row with no A or two, or letters without the key. Reason: the RAM exists so only one person
  is accountable for any task (printed 317, PDF 356).

### D2. Estimating technique and alternatives for each resource

- **When:** 9.2, at planning and "periodically throughout the project as needed" (printed 320, PDF 359); trigger:
  scope, activity list or assumptions change (form 2.28 prose, printed 113, PDF 124).
- **Techniques (printed 324 to 325, PDF 363 to 364):** bottom-up (per activity, aggregated up); analogous (a similar
  past project; quick; usable when only top WBS levels are known); parametric (algorithm or statistical relation
  on historical data; accuracy depends on the model and its data); expert judgment; meetings with functional
  managers. Alternatives analysis picks among capability levels, machine types, manual or automated tools,
  make-rent-or-buy, "within the defined constraints"; each option's trade-off: not stated in the book.
- **Inputs:** activity list and attributes, assumption log, cost estimates, resource calendars, risk register,
  scope baseline, 2.26 (9.2.1). Here: dictionary Resource and Labor Hours, assumptions 9, 10, 17, budget lines 2, 3.
- **Recorded:** 2.28 Quantity, Assumptions, Comments (basis); 1.2 Assumption Log (9.2.3.4, printed 327, PDF 366);
  3.2 for a make-rent-or-buy choice.
- **Formula (book example, no named formula, printed 324, PDF 363):** "if an activity needs 4,000 hours of coding
  and it needs to finish it in 1 year, it will require two people to code (each doing 2,000 hours a year)". Read
  as people = effort hours / hours one person gives in the period, 4,000 / 2,000 = 2. For availability and skill,
  see D9.
- **Wrong move:** a quantity with no basis, or choosing an option without its quality and availability effects.
  Reason: the basis must give "a clear and complete understanding of how the resource estimate was derived"
  (printed 326, PDF 365); poor material, late equipment and excess inventory each cost quality, time or money
  (printed 310, PDF 349).

### D3. How a role is acquired

- **When:** 9.3 whenever a resource must be obtained: M0 mobilisation, the mobile developer, any replacement (R4).
- **Techniques:** pre-assignment (printed 333, PDF 372); multicriteria decision analysis with weighted criteria,
  availability, cost, ability, and for people experience, knowledge, skills, attitude, international factors
  (printed 332, PDF 371); negotiation with functional managers, other projects, external organisations (printed
  332 to 333, PDF 371 to 372); virtual teams widen the pool but strain communication (printed 333, PDF 372; 311,
  350). Internal people come from functional or resource managers, external ones through procurement (printed
  329, PDF 368).
- **Stated trade-off:** failing to acquire hits schedule, budget, satisfaction, quality and risk, at worst
  cancellation; an alternative resource of other competence or cost is allowed unless a legal, regulatory or
  mandatory criterion is broken (printed 330, PDF 369).
- **Inputs:** 2.26, cost baseline, schedule, calendars, requirements, 1.3. Here: charter "Resources preassigned",
  assumptions 10, 17, 18, R3, R4.
- **Recorded:** 2.26 Staff Acquisition or Resource Acquisition; 2.28 Assumptions; 2.32 new risks; 3.2; 3.3 when
  the cost baseline or schedule moves (9.3.3.4 to 9.3.3.5, printed 334, PDF 373).
- **Formula:** none.
- **Wrong move:** swapping in other skills without recording the effect. Reason: the manager "will be required to
  document the impact" on schedule, budget, risks, quality, training (printed 330, PDF 369).

### D4. Team development approach and spend

- **When:** 9.4, "throughout the project" (printed 336, PDF 375); trigger: a newcomer, a stalled Tuckman stage
  (printed 338, PDF 377), a skill gap.
- **Techniques and trade-offs (printed 340 to 342, PDF 379 to 381):** colocation (temporary or whole project);
  virtual team and communication technology (reduced costs, more skilled resources, 340, PDF 379; challenges mainly in communication, 311, PDF 350); team building (essential
  early, continuous, "particularly valuable" when remote); recognition and rewards (effective only if they meet a
  need the person values; money tangible, intangible equal or better; recognise throughout, mind culture);
  training (formal or informal; cost in the project budget or borne by the organisation if reusable); assessments.
- **Inputs:** 2.26, 2.27, lessons learned, schedule, calendars (9.4.1). Here: no development plan yet.
- **Recorded:** 2.26 Training Requirements, Rewards and Recognition, Team Development; cost estimate, and 3.3 if
  the cost baseline moves; results in 3.7 and 3.5. Reward size and frequency: not stated in the book.
- **Formula:** none.
- **Wrong move:** one reward for all, or only at the end. Reason: rewards work only if they satisfy a need that
  person values, and recognition belongs throughout the life cycle (printed 341 to 342, PDF 380 to 381).

### D5. Handling a conflict and when to escalate

- **When:** 9.5; trigger: scarce resources, scheduling priorities, work styles (printed 348, PDF 387).
- **Techniques (printed 349, PDF 388):** withdraw or avoid (postpones); smooth or accommodate (harmony, concedes);
  compromise or reconcile (partial satisfaction, "occasionally results in a lose-lose situation"); force or direct
  (win-lose, for an emergency); collaborate or problem solve (consensus, can be win-win). Factors: importance and
  intensity, time pressure, relative power, the relationship, short or long term (printed 348, PDF 387).
- **Sequence:** team members first; the project manager facilitates if it escalates; early, usually in private;
  formal procedures, even discipline, if disruption continues. Days before escalation: not stated in the book.
- **Inputs:** 2.27 process, issue log, assignments. Here: charter conflict rule, five working days then sponsor.
- **Recorded:** 2.27 Conflict Resolution Process (rule); 3.1 Issue Log (case; 9.5.3.3); 3.2 (outcome).
- **Formula:** none.
- **Wrong move:** forcing by default or staying silent. Reason: force is win-lose, avoiding only postpones, and
  conflict "should be addressed early" (printed 348 to 349, PDF 387 to 388).

### D6. Whether and what to assess in the team

- **When:** 9.4 after development efforts; interval not stated in the book.
- **Technique:** team performance assessment with the 9.4.3.1 indicators (skills, team competencies, turnover,
  cohesiveness; printed 343, PDF 382). Criteria "may" sit in the plan (printed 339, PDF 378); thresholds: not
  stated in the book.
- **Inputs:** 2.26 criteria, 4.1 reports, issue log, ongoing assessments (9.5.1.4, printed 347, PDF 386).
- **Recorded:** 3.7 ratings, Comments, Areas for Development; 2.27 or 2.26 updated (9.4.3.4, printed 344,
  PDF 383); 3.3 if actions change the plan (9.4.3.2).
- **Formula:** none.
- **Wrong move:** ratings with no stated criteria. Reason: Table 3.7 asks for comments "that justify the rating"
  (printed 185, PDF 196).

### D7. Staffing change or too little capacity: who decides

- **When:** 9.5, 9.3 or 9.6; trigger: a member leaves or moves, or load or use departs from plan. Staffing
  changes "can disrupt the project team", slip the schedule or exceed the budget (printed 350, PDF 389).
- **Techniques in this chapter:** acquire an additional or alternative resource (D3); alternatives analysis of
  overtime or additional team resources against late or phased delivery, with cost-benefit analysis (9.6.2.1,
  printed 356, PDF 395, written for physical resources; the team side is 9.5); trend analysis; six-step problem
  solving (printed 356, PDF 395); negotiation, influencing (printed 357, PDF 396). Per-option cost: not stated.
- **Outside this chapter (see `pmbok-schedule.md`):** resource leveling may change the critical path, smoothing
  uses float only (6.5.2.3, printed 211, PDF 250); crashing adds resources or overtime at "increased risk and/or
  cost", fast tracking risks rework, both "without reducing the project scope" (6.5.2.6, printed 215, PDF 254).
- **Inputs:** 2.18 loading, `tools/level.py`, cost baseline, reserve limits.
- **Recorded:** 3.3 (Cost, Schedule, Comments listing alternatives) and 3.2; issue in 3.1. Schedule or cost
  baseline changes "can be approved only through the Perform Integrated Change Control" (printed 354, PDF 393).
  Reassigning work inside the team is the project manager's own authority (charter).
- **Formula:** none in this chapter.
- **Wrong move:** shrinking estimates or editing a baseline to fit people or date. Reason: compression keeps the
  scope (printed 215, PDF 254) and any plan change "goes through the organization's change control process via a
  change request" (printed 351, PDF 390).

### D8. Team charter: who writes it and when it reopens

- **When:** 9.1 at mobilisation; reopen when members join or turnover is substantial (printed 320, PDF 359;
  form prose printed 109, PDF 120).
- **Technique:** the team develops it or at least contributes (printed 320, PDF 359); early clear guidelines cut
  misunderstandings. The decision rule states the project manager's relative power, any voting, and when a
  decision is revisited (Table 2.25, printed 110, PDF 121).
- **Inputs:** 9.1 inputs; here charter pre-assignments, authority level, conflict rule.
- **Recorded:** 2.27 boxes and signatures; 3.2 for amendments.
- **Formula:** none.
- **Wrong move:** the project manager writes it alone. Reason: it "works best when the team develops it".

### D9. How many people, and how long, from effort

- **When:** 9.2 and the duration step (6.4); trigger: effort known, or quantity or availability changes.
- **Technique:** parametric estimating (D2) with the Book of Forms worksheet.
- **Inputs:** effort hours (dictionary), percent available (assumption 10 for customer staff; no calendars),
  performance factor.
- **Recorded:** 2.28 Quantity and Comments; 2.17 holds the duration.
- **Formula, as printed (form 2.17, Table 2.16, PDF page 86, printed page 75):**
  `Effort / ( quantity x percent available x performance factor ) = duration`. Effort in hours; quantity is the
  number of resources; percent available is the share of time available per day or week; performance factor 1.0
  average, 0.8 highly skilled, 1.2 less skilled; duration in hours. Book example: 150 / ( 2 x .75 x .8 ) = 125
  hours. People are productive on project work about 75 percent of the time (form 2.17 prose, PDF page 84,
  printed page 73).
- **Trap:** the text says to multiply effort by .8 for a skilled resource (shorter), but the printed equation
  divides by .8 (longer; the printed 125 follows it). Apply it as printed, name the reading in Comments, tell the
  team; do not pick silently.
- **Wrong move:** treating a person as 100 percent available. Reason: the worksheet divides by percent available
  and gives 75 percent as typical.
