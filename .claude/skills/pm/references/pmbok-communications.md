## Chapter terms worth using, with the book's meaning

- **Two parts** (printed 359 and 362, PDF 398 and 401): a strategy built on the needs of the project and its
  stakeholders, then the communications that carry it out. 10.1 plans, 10.2 carries out, 10.3 checks.
- **Dimensions** (printed 361, PDF 400): internal or external; formal or informal; upward, downward or horizontal;
  official or unofficial; written or oral. A plan row says which applies.
- **Interactive, push, pull** (10.1.2.5, printed 374, PDF 413): interactive is real-time and multidirectional
  (meetings, calls); push goes to named recipients and "does not ensure" it reached or was understood (memos,
  reports, email); pull suits large or complex information and large audiences, the recipient fetches it (portals,
  repositories). Small group communication means about three to six people.
- **Communication models** (10.1.2.4, printed 371 to 373, PDF 410 to 412): basic (encode, transmit, decode; aims at
  delivery, not understanding); interactive (adds acknowledge and feedback); cross-cultural (adds the parties'
  state, culture and biases as noise; not for email, broadcast or social media). The sender answers for clarity and
  confirmation; the receiver for full receipt, correct interpretation and acknowledgment (printed 372, PDF 411).
- **Communication requirements analysis** (10.1.2.2, printed 369 to 370, PDF 408 to 409): type and format of
  information combined with the value of that information. Listed sources include the number of potential channels;
  no channel-count formula is given (not stated in the book).
- **Technology factors** (10.1.2.3, printed 370 to 371, PDF 409 to 410): urgency, availability and reliability, ease
  of use, project environment, sensitivity and confidentiality.
- **Project reporting** (10.2.2.5, printed 385, PDF 424): distribute information at the level, format and detail
  each stakeholder type needs, regularly or on an exception basis. Work performance reports are the output of 4.5.
- **Work performance data, information, report** (4.3.3.2 printed 95, 4.5.1.3 printed 109, 4.5.3.1 printed 112; PDF
  134, 148, 151): data are raw observations (work completed, actual dates, actual costs, defect counts);
  information is data compared with the plan and other variables; a report is that information written down "to
  generate decisions, actions, or awareness".
- **Corrective, preventive, defect repair** (4.5.3.2, printed 112, PDF 151): corrective realigns performance with the
  plan; preventive keeps future performance aligned; defect repair modifies a nonconforming product. Raised as
  change requests (4.3.3.4, printed 96, PDF 135).
- **Stakeholder engagement assessment matrix** (13.2.2.5, printed 521 to 522, PDF 560 to 561): current against
  desired engagement (Unaware, Resistant, Neutral, Supportive, Leading); the gap "will direct the level of
  communications". Reused in 10.1.2.7 and 10.3.2.3 (printed 376 and 392, PDF 415 and 431).
- **Escalation process** is a plan component (10.1.3.1, printed 377, PDF 416); no technique or threshold (not stated).

## Traps

- **F08 and F09 are product functions.** F09 "Notification and internal communication" and F08 "progress reports"
  are what the system does for the center. They are not the project's communications management plan or status
  reports. Do not copy F09 channels (email, SMS, push) into 2.30 as project channels.
- **4.1 and 4.2 come from 4.5, not from 10.2.** 10.2.2.5 says work performance reports are the output of Monitor and
  Control Project Work (printed 385, PDF 424). 10.2 distributes them.
- **No work package funds team member status reports.** 1.1.1.3 prices only the project manager's weekly report (84
  hours, 21 weeks). A 4.1 per team member per week is extra work; propose it as a decision, do not assume it.
- **A stated number of weeks versus the levelled schedule.** If the dictionary states a report estimate in weeks and the
  2.18 window of that activity is longer (or a pending 3.3 changes the window), the estimate no longer covers the
  reporting period. Report the difference, do not rescale the estimate.
- **Reporting period.** The weekly report is the 4.1 and 4.2 period. The charter's cost variance limit is read at each stage gate
  (charter), so a weekly 4.2 shows the running figure and the gate decides.
- **Term for the reserve.** Charter Part 2B page 2, Cost row, says "management reserve use reported monthly"; the
  money is a contingency reserve (budget line 6). Use contingency reserve (house-style.md).
- **Where the reserve sits.** Budget line 6 is held at project level, outside the 78 work packages (scope package
  rollup). The book defines a contingency reserve as budget within the cost baseline for identified risks, which
  "can be provided at any level" from activity to project (7.2.2.6, printed 245, PDF 284), and keeps management
  reserves outside the baseline (7.3.3.1, printed 254, PDF 293). State which BAC an earned value figure uses.
- **A 4.6 for a supplier that is not a contractor.** The project is itself the supplier under the center's contract.
  Do not fill 4.6 for the gateway or hosting provider without a contract of the project's own.

## Decisions in this area

Each decision: when; techniques and trade-offs; inputs and where kept; form and field; formula; wrong move.
"Not stated in the book" means chapter 10, 4.5 and 12.3 give no value; the project value comes from the charter.

### D1. Who needs what information (stakeholder rows)
- **When:** Plan Communications Management, early, during stakeholder identification and plan development (printed
  367, PDF 406); again when the community changes or a phase starts.
- **Technique:** communication requirements analysis (10.1.2.2): combine the type and format of information with the
  value of that information; the book states no trade-off and no cut-off. Stakeholder engagement assessment matrix
  (10.1.2.7): where current engagement is below desired, plan extra communication beyond the regular reports.
- **Inputs:** charter key stakeholder list, 1.3 (not yet in docs/forms), requirements documentation, resource plan.
- **Record:** 2.30 Stakeholder, Information; 1.3 register updated (10.1.3.3).
- **Formula:** none; the channel-count formula is not in the chapter (not stated in the book).
- **Wrong move:** one report to everyone. Reason: information needs "may vary widely" and project reporting adapts
  level and detail to each stakeholder type (printed 367, 385; PDF 406, 424).

### D2. Method, technology, timing and confidentiality per row
- **When:** Plan Communications Management, filling Method, Timing or Frequency and Constraints; revisited by D8.
- **Technique:** interactive, push or pull (10.1.2.5) chosen with the technology factors (10.1.2.3). Stated
  trade-offs: interactive is real time and multidirectional; push assures distribution only; pull needs the
  recipient to act. Timing: urgency, frequency and format vary by project and phase (printed 370, PDF 409); no
  interval is given (not stated in the book). Confidential content: "additional security measures" and a named
  person who authorizes release (printed 371 and 377, PDF 410 and 416).
- **Inputs:** stakeholder availability (assumption 10), policies on security (10.1.1.5), tools in use (not stated in
  project docs); charter cadence: weekly report from M0, gate reviews M1 to M7, demos M3 and M4, monthly reserve
  and overdue figures.
- **Record:** 2.30 Method, Timing or Frequency (with the acknowledgment time), Constraints (authorizer named there,
  no box); dates agree with 2.18 (alignment, PDF 130, printed 119).
- **Wrong move:** treating a sent report as understood, or never revisiting the plan. Reasons: push "does not ensure"
  it reached or was understood (printed 374, PDF 413); the plan is reviewed regularly and revised when the
  community changes or a phase starts (printed 367, PDF 406).

### D3. Level of detail of a report (exception or regular)
- **When:** designing 4.1 and 4.2, and the template fixed at M1 (1.1.1.3 Technical Information).
- **Technique:** project reporting (10.2.2.5): adapt level, format and detail; prepare regularly or on exception.
  The book states both options and no trade-off between them; an exception report needs a threshold, and variance
  thresholds belong to the plan (4.5.1.3, printed 109, PDF 148).
- **Record:** 4.2 sections per Table 4.2; presentation (dashboard, stop light) is a Tailoring Tip (PDF 208, printed 197).
- **Wrong move:** reporting raw data. Reason: data become useful only when compared with the budget, work, resources
  and funding schedule (4.5.1.3, printed 109, PDF 148).

### D4. Does a variance need action, and which kind
- **When:** each reporting cycle of Monitor and Control Project Work, when a compared figure leaves its threshold.
- **Technique (4.5.2.2, printed 111, PDF 150):** variance analysis (planned against actual, integrated across cost,
  time, technical and resource), trend analysis (forecast from past results, recommends preventive action), root
  cause analysis (why), earned value analysis, alternatives analysis (choose among corrective actions or
  combinations), cost-benefit analysis (best corrective action by cost).
- **Inputs:** basis of estimates, issue log, risk register and report, cost and schedule forecasts, milestone list
  (4.5.1.2, printed 108, PDF 147); thresholds: the charter's cost and milestone-slip criteria.
- **Record:** 4.1 Root Cause, Planned Corrective or Preventive Action; 4.2 same, two copies; action that changes a
  baseline: 3.3 and 3.4, decision in 3.2.
- **Formulas:** see below. Percent variance is not defined in the book; write the base used in the template.
- **Wrong move:** closing the gap by cutting estimates. Reason: only approved changes enter a revised baseline
  (printed 117, PDF 156); the route is a change request (3.3) to the sponsor.

### D5. Forecast method, and whether the BAC is still reachable
- **When:** cost forecasts each period; cumulative CPI falls below the baseline; the BAC looks no longer viable.
- **Technique (7.4.2.2, printed 264 to 265, PDF 303 to 304):** bottom-up (most common, builds on actual cost plus a
  new estimate to complete); at the budgeted rate (accepts results to date, assumes future work at budget); at
  present CPI (experience continues); CPI and SPI together (most useful when schedule drives the effort; weights such
  as 80/20 or 50/50 are the manager's judgment). Compare the manual figure with the calculated range.
- **Reachability:** TCPI against BAC or EAC (7.4.2.3, printed 266, PDF 305): above 1.0 is harder to complete;
  whether it is achievable is "a judgment call" on risk, time remaining and technical performance; an approved EAC
  replaces BAC in the formula.
- **Record:** 4.2 Impact to Overall Budget or Contingency Funds; 4.4 holds the figures; a baseline that must move
  goes to 3.3 (Cost, Schedule).
- **Formula:** EAC variants and TCPI below.
- **Wrong move:** assuming recovery. Reason: future improvement "should be accepted only when supported by project
  risk analysis" (printed 265, PDF 304).

### D6. Contingency reserve: use, release or request more
- **When:** a response to R1 to R12 is triggered, or a risk passes without occurring.
- **Technique:** reserve analysis (7.4.2.2, printed 265, PDF 304): monitor whether contingency and management reserves
  are still needed; unused contingency may be removed; new risk analysis may justify a request. Contingency is
  budget within the cost baseline for identified risks (7.2.2.6, printed 245, PDF 284). Charter: draw only against
  the risks and the change control the charter names; escalate use above the reserve limit the charter gives.
- **Record:** 4.2 Impact to Overall Budget or Contingency Funds; 3.3 Cost; 3.2.
- **Wrong move:** spending reserve on a variance with no identified risk behind it (reason: 7.2.2.6 above).

### D7. Escalate, and to whom
- **When:** a charter trigger fires (D4 thresholds, R3, R8, R9, five working days of unresolved conflict).
- **Technique:** the plan carries escalation processes (10.1.3.1); a Tailoring Tip adds an escalation field to 4.1,
  4.2 and 4.6. The book gives no threshold (not stated in the book); the charter does.
- **Record:** an added escalation field, else Comments, stating this in the reading paragraph; decision in 3.2.
- **Wrong move:** sitting on a forecast overrun beyond the charter's percent; the book gives no reason, the charter requires escalation.

### D8. Change communication, or change to the plan itself
- **When:** Monitor Communications finds the plan not working (surveys, issue log, observation, engagement gaps;
  printed 389, PDF 428); issues, KPIs, risks or conflicts "may trigger an immediate revision".
- **Technique:** stakeholder engagement assessment matrix (10.3.2.3); observation and conversation (10.3.2.4).
  Plan changes go through change control as a change request (10.3.3.3, printed 393, PDF 432); the change log
  tells impacted stakeholders of approved, deferred and rejected changes (10.2.1.2, printed 382, PDF 421).
- **Record:** 3.3 and 3.4; revised rows in 2.30; 3.2 for the decision.
- **Wrong move:** editing a baselined plan quietly. Reason: once baselined, changes go through integrated change
  control, and baselines change only from the last baseline forward (printed 115 and 120, PDF 154 and 159).

### D9. Contractor performance and claims (4.6)
- **When:** a contract of the project exists and a period closes. None exists yet.
- **Technique (12.3.2, printed 498, PDF 537):** performance reviews, earned value analysis, trend analysis,
  inspection, audits, claims administration (settle by negotiation where possible; ADR is the contract's route).
- **Inputs:** agreements, procurement documentation, work performance data (12.3.1, printed 495 to 496, PDF 534 to
  535). **Record:** 4.6 Cost, Schedule, Claims or Disputes; contract changes written and approved first
  (12.3.1.5, printed 496, PDF 535), entered through 3.3.
- **Wrong move:** a cost forecast on a fixed price contract. Reason: Table 4.6 says do not enter one (form 4.6,
  PDF 225, printed 214).

### Formulas the book gives (Table 7-1, printed 267, PDF 306; 7.4.2.2 printed 262 to 266)
Variables: PV planned value, the authorized budget for scheduled work to the data date; EV earned value, the budget
of the work completed; AC actual cost of the work completed; BAC budget at completion; EAC estimate at completion;
ETC estimate to complete. All are money (VND here); CPI, SPI and TCPI are unitless ratios.
- `SV = EV - PV` (above 0 ahead, 0 on schedule, below 0 behind)
- `CV = EV - AC` (above 0 under planned cost, below 0 over)
- `SPI = EV/PV`; `CPI = EV/AC` (above 1.0 ahead or under cost, below 1.0 behind or over)
- `VAC = BAC - EAC`
- `EAC = AC + Bottom-up ETC`; `EAC = AC + (BAC - EV)`; `EAC = BAC / CPI`; `EAC = AC + [(BAC - EV) / (CPI x SPI)]`
- `ETC = EAC - AC`, or `ETC = Reestimate`
- `TCPI = (BAC - EV) / (BAC - AC)`; with an approved EAC: `TCPI = (BAC - EV) / (EAC - AC)`
Not in the book: a percent-variance formula; a communication channel count formula; any reporting interval.
