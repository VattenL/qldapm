# 2.23 Quality Management Plan

*Form 2.23, A Project Manager's Book of Forms, 3rd edition, PDF pages 108 to 109 (printed pages 97 to 98); element descriptions in Table 2.21, PDF pages 106 to 107 (printed pages 95 to 96). The plan is the output of PMBOK 6 section 8.1 Plan Quality Management, and carries the components listed in PMBOK 6 section 8.1.3.1; the quality tools of that list, which the form prints no box for, are named inside the two approach boxes. It is built from the charter and the scope package as committed on 30 September 2026. Of the documents the book says the plan must align with, the charter and the scope baseline exist; there is no scope, requirements, or resource management plan and no procurement document yet, so this plan cites the charter and the WBS dictionary directly. It follows the baselined charter, not the scope change request of 8 October 2026, which has no disposition yet; if the sponsor approves that request, the rows for A01, A02, A09, and A12 and the deliverables D4 and D11 change with it. Quality Objectives holds one row for each acceptance criterion of charter section 1.3, its text copied as written, and two rows for the warranty response targets and the review rule; the printed form draws four numbered lines, and the table keeps one row per objective instead. Quality Roles and Responsibilities likewise holds one row per role. Deliverables and Processes Subject to Quality Review is one tall box on the printed form, so its two cells list their entries inline as (1), (2), and so on. A WBS code such as 1.6.1.4 names the work package of the scope package that does the work. <mark>Highlighted</mark> content is a proposal or an assumption that no project document confirms yet.*

**Quality Objectives**

| Metric or Specification | Measure |

| **Quality Management Approach** | Quality is planned into the work rather than inspected in at the end, the preference PMBOK 6 states for prevention over inspection. (1) Design and both construction iterations each carry their own assurance control account (1.3.2, 1.4.3, 1.5.5), and requirements carry a peer review (1.2.2.2), so reviews and tests are scheduled and costed work packages, and no author reviews their own work. (2) The exception behaviour is specified with the functions (1.2.1.4) and becomes the negative test cases, so failure paths are designed in rather than found in UAT. (3) The build pipeline of 1.2.3.1 runs the build and the test suite on every commit from before M2. (4) Quality reports: the consolidated defect log with its trend report (1.6.2.1), the performance, capacity, and security reports (1.6.1.4, 1.6.1.5), and <mark>the count of open Critical and High defects and open review findings in each weekly status report (1.1.1.2)</mark>. <mark>Tools used: check sheets for test execution, a histogram of defects by area and severity in the trend report, and root cause analysis for every Critical defect.</mark> (5) Audit timing and content: the milestone gate reviews at M1 to M7 (1.1.1.2) check each gate's evidence against this plan; <mark>in addition, one process audit on form 3.6 before the UAT entry review at M5, checking that the reviews, the defect log, the severity rule, and the regression cycle were followed as this plan states; no work package of the scope package funds it, so the sponsor decides whether to commission it.</mark> (6) Findings of reviews, tests, and the audit feed the lessons learned written in the closeout report (1.10.2.1). |
| **Quality Control Approach** | The product is measured against the acceptance criteria, and the results are the quality control measurements that decide each gate. (1) Inspection: requirement, design, and code reviews record their findings in a finding list that is closed before the gate (1.2.2.2, 1.3.2.2, 1.4.3.3, 1.5.5.3). (2) Testing at the levels of the test plan: unit, integration, system, performance, capacity, security, and acceptance, on web, Android, and iOS; system tests run on staging with migrated sample data. (3) Measurement: performance is reported as the 95th percentile against each A02 threshold (1.6.1.4); A03 by loading the stated volumes; A04 by testing endpoint authorisation against the role matrix (1.6.1.5); A06 by the restore drills during UAT and during warranty (1.8.1.2, 1.10.1.1) <mark>and by availability as measured monthly by the center's hosting monitoring once it is live</mark>; A09 by a test matrix over every browser and device it names. (4) Gates: no open Critical or High defect in F01 to F03 at the M3 demo (1.4.3.1); the feature-complete criteria for every function of iteration 2 at M4 (1.5.5.1, 1.5.5.2); the UAT entry criteria of the test plan reviewed at M5 (1.6.2.2); UAT recorded per test case and signed against A01 (1.8.3.1), which is the point where the verified deliverables pass to Validate Scope. (5) A pass rate below 99%, or any open Critical or High defect, means go-live is not authorised, and the charter's failure path applies. |
| **Applicable Quality Procedures** | (1) Nonconformance and rework: every defect is logged in the single project defect log with a severity from the definitions of the test plan; it is fixed in the defect-fixing packages and verified by the regression cycle of 1.6.1.8; a deliverable that fails its acceptance is reworked and presented again before its gate. (2) Corrective actions: a nonconformance whose correction changes scope, schedule, or cost is raised as a change request through 1.1.1.3 into Perform Integrated Change Control, PMBOK 6 section 4.6; if UAT does not pass, the project manager puts the charter's three options to the sponsor within two working days. (3) Quality audits: <mark>the audit of the Quality Management Approach, on form 3.6, its findings tracked to closure in the issue log</mark>. (4) Continuous improvement: the plan-do-check-act cycle at each gate, where review and test findings show which process let a defect through; the change is made to the next phase's process, and recorded in the lessons learned of the closeout report. |
